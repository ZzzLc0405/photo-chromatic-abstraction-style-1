# Validated Style-1 examples and failure tests

## Desert: palette stack plus four circles

- Preserve the broad blue/orange chromatic opposition.
- Compress sand into an ordered orange-to-brown sequence.
- Represent the non-dominant contrasting event with four unevenly placed circles.
- Use small gaps between related palette bands.
- Lesson: several accents can remain one restrained visual language.

## Tunisia vehicle scene: large blue field plus one circle

- Ignore vehicle anatomy, wheels, person detail, and terrain texture.
- Preserve dominant blue, pale ground, and a small number of horizontal transitions.
- Use one large circle as a source-derived interruption rather than rebuilding the vehicle.
- Lesson: semantic subjects may disappear completely.

## Salt landscape: bands plus triangle and circle

- Compress the scene into muted horizontal fields.
- Use one triangle for the mound and one circle for a secondary color event.
- Keep both shapes subordinate to the palette.
- Lesson: two accent families are acceptable only when both are source-justified and the composition stays color-first.

## Sea: blue bands plus one large circle

- Reduce sea and sky to a restrained blue value sequence.
- Use one pale circle to break the band system.
- Delete wave texture, cloud detail, and literal horizon effects.
- Lesson: a circle may function as chromatic punctuation rather than a literal sun or moon.

## Grassland with a broad cloud bank

- Preserve blue sky and the green field sequence as the chromatic base.
- If the cloud bank is selected as the accent source, represent its breadth with 3-5 unequal circles or ellipses rather than one isolated token.
- Stagger height, scale, and overlap lightly; do not align the circles into an icon row.
- Keep small terrain detail and literal cloud contours out.
- Lesson: accent count and arrangement adapt to the perceived extent and rhythm of the source event.

## Winter cyclist: rejected and corrected logic

- Reject a literal or symbolic reconstruction of the cyclist and bicycle when they contribute little color weight.
- Preserve blue sky, pale horizon, warm distant snow, gray-violet vegetation, blue-gray snow, and dark road.
- Compare the visible tree rhythm against minor clouds and the tiny person. When the trees carry substantially more area, repetition, and positional influence, derive the accent family from the trees and omit the person.
- Reduce the trees to a restrained repeated rectangular family or another canonical non-literal form; do not draw branches or trunks.
- Lesson: the accent comes from the strongest perceptual event, not the easiest shape or narrative subject.

## Failure tests

### Too literal

- Vehicle body plus wheels plus lights
- Person split into head, torso, pants, and shoes
- Tree silhouettes, wires, lamps, signs, or window frames retained for completeness

Fix: return to color weight and reduce the retained memory to one canonical accent family.

### Too generic

- A reusable stack of bands with no source-derived accent
- Colors sampled without preserving warm/cool hierarchy or dominant weight

Fix: identify one genuine source event and create 1-5 restrained accent instances.

### Overdecorated

- More than five accent instances
- Three or more accent families
- Every accent uses a new color
- Randomly scattered shapes with no shared relation

Fix: keep one family, reduce count, reuse palette colors, and restore a clear group relation.

### Overregulated

- Every band separated by identical white lines
- Every repeated circle equal in size and equally spaced
- Accent always centered

Fix: make gaps, size, spacing, height, and boundary contact adaptive to the source and composition.
